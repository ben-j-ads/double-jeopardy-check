How it works

Double jeopardy

Within a category, smaller brands have fewer buyers, and those buyers also buy the brand a little less often. Bigger brands get both: more buyers and slightly more loyal ones. Ehrenberg and colleagues documented this across hundreds of categories (the "double jeopardy" law), and it is why raising loyalty is a poor way to grow a small brand.

The test

For each brand, a straight line is fitted through the other brands (purchases per buyer against penetration). The brand's prediction interval at its own penetration is the normal range.

fit: w = a + b x penetration (ordinary least squares, other brands only) range: w_hat +/- t x s x sqrt(1 + 1/n + (x0 - xbar)^2 / Sxx)

Read this before you act on the verdict

A handful of brands gives a loose line. With 5 or 6 brands the normal range is wide, and nearly everything looks normal. That is the honest answer, not a failure.

The pattern is empirical. It describes typical categories. Niche brands and brands in partitioned markets can differ for real reasons, such as a segment that buys less often in total.

Check the data first. Penetration and frequency must come from the same panel and period for every brand.
